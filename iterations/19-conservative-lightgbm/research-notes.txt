Research shortlist requested 2026-11-21; literature screening, not reproduction.

1. Bergmeir, Hyndman & Benitez (2016), Bagging exponential smoothing methods
using STL decomposition and Box-Cox transformation.
https://robjhyndman.com/publications/bagging-ets/
Paper: moving-block bootstrap of decomposition remainders; combine ETS forecasts.
Evidence is M3, especially monthly data, not this sparse weekly pharmacy panel.
Project inference: dependence-preserving bootstrap deserves consideration after
current review; arbitrary independent Gaussian noise can destroy zeros and bursts.

2. Petropoulos, Hyndman & Bergmeir (2018), Exploring the sources of uncertainty:
why does bagging for time series forecasting work?
https://robjhyndman.com/papers/exploring-sources-uncertainty.pdf
Paper finds model uncertainty dominant in its ETS/ARIMA competition-series study.
Project inference: current diverse ensemble already exploits this idea; extra
randomness or seeds need not improve bias or beat it. Earlier D33 three-seed
results in iteration 02 were mixed, not evidence for automatic bagging promotion.

3. Salinas et al., DeepAR (2020 journal version).
https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110
Global probabilistic autoregressive forecasting across related series.
Project proposal: a compact negative-binomial count model, simulate full 13-week
paths, take marginal median at each week for absolute-error scoring. Simulation
is an approximation to the model distribution, not new information; more paths
reduce sampling error, not model misspecification. Fit and tune on past only.
Historical on_hand is allowed, future observed stock must not be supplied.
Count-likelihood assumption needs checking against processed fractional targets.

4. Turkmen et al. (2020 preprint), Intermittent Demand Forecasting with Renewal Processes.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01550
Models inter-demand times and sizes, including aging/clustering/quasi-periodicity.
Project inference: plausible sparse-demand challenger; greater change and effort
than current regularization. Distinct from iteration 15 probability gate, whose
small gains did not pass review. Avoid simply repeating that failed gate.

5. Chen et al. (2023 preprint), FrAug: Frequency Domain Augmentation for Time Series Forecasting.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09292
Forecast-specific frequency-domain augmentation evaluated with deep models.
Project inference: low priority; has no demonstrated benefit on our zero-heavy
weekly panel or LightGBM. Perturbing historical inputs alone need not preserve
correct conditional labels. No transfer of reported paper gains assumed.

Metric implication derived from project score: per-series scale fixed at forecast
origin, so a conditional marginal median minimizes expected absolute error per
week. Do not average simulated demand paths and assume the resulting mean is
MASE optimal. A mixture-distribution median is not generally the mean or median
of component point forecasts. Weekly competition errors differ from errors on
13-week summed inventory demand; do not optimize only the horizon total.

All methods remain proposals pending user experiment choice. Current approved
scope is iteration 19 only. No new model, perturbation or Monte Carlo run here.
